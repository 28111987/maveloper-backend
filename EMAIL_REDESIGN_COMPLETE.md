## 5. THE TWO LOGOS, AND WHAT HAPPENS WHEN IMAGES ARE BLOCKED

Both are wired as remote images. I read their real dimensions off the GIF headers
rather than assuming:

| | URL | Real size | Drawn at | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Masthead | `…/Maveloper%20Logo%20Animation.gif` | **600 × 120** | 170 × 34 (exact 5:1) | 236 KB |
| Footer | `…/DesignMavlers%20Logo%20Animation…gif` | **500 × 500** — square | 60 × 60 | 589 KB |

The footer mark is **square, not a wordmark**. A 132 × 30 letterbox — which is
what I would have used had I guessed — would have squashed it.

**Blocked images, handled five ways:**

1. **The alt text reads as the wordmark.** `alt="MAVELOPER"` and
   `alt="DESIGN MAVLERS"` — not "logo", not a filename.
2. **The alt text is styled.** Inline `font-family` / `size` / `weight` /
   `letter-spacing` / `color` on the `<img>` itself is what Gmail, Apple Mail and
   Outlook.com use to draw alt text, so the blocked state renders as a wordmark
   rather than 8px system serif. Masthead alt is ink-on-lime; footer alt is house
   yellow.
3. **The box is reserved.** `width` AND `height` **attributes** (not only styles)
   on both images, plus a fixed-height container cell on the masthead. The band
   keeps its height and nothing reflows.
4. **The wordmark is never only an image.** The masthead carries a live-text
   scope label and a live-text 48px title; the footer carries the live-text line
   `Maveloper · CRAFTED BY MAVLERS` directly under the logo. **Blocked images cost
   the animation, never the identity.** This matters most in the footer, where a
   60px square box genuinely cannot hold the words "DESIGN MAVLERS" at a readable
   size — the live-text line is what carries it there.
5. **They stay remote.** Base64-inlining a 236 KB and a 589 KB animated GIF would
   add ~1.1 MB of encoded bytes to a message whose whole point is now to sit far
   under Gmail's clip threshold.

Outlook 2007-2019 draws frame 1 of a GIF and does not animate. Expected, not a
defect. ★ **Worth your attention: 589 KB is heavy for a footer mark on mobile
data.** A static PNG or a shorter loop would be kinder; I did not re-encode your
assets without being asked.

## 6. THE DESIGN

Built in the vocabulary the quarterly leadership brief uses, by name.

> and `emHeadScoped_` across `maveloper-backend`, `maveloper-os-current`, the

### Ported

- **`emKick_`** — 28px gradient rule → uppercase letterspaced 10px label →
  optional scope chip. Three sections, three kickers.
- **`emHeadScoped_`** — 23px / 800 statement under each kicker.
- **`emCap_`** — 13.5px, `max-width:520px`, 22px leading.
- **`emSec_`** — three bands, each a solid `bgcolor` (what Outlook draws) plus a
  radial glow (what everything else draws), 1px top border, 50px/46px padding.
- **the masthead** — gradient panel, logo left, letterspaced label right, 48px title.
- **the footer** — 2.5px gradient rule, then a black panel with the logo.
- **`qbGradText_`** — the per-character ramp.
- **`Poppins,'Segoe UI',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif`** — the second face is the point.
- **`@media (max-width:600px)`** with `col2` / `col2b` stacking.
- **`[data-ogsc]`** and `prefers-color-scheme` via `em_ondark` / `em_ondark_ink`.

**Not ported: `col3` and `col4`.** Nothing in an eleven-field email is three- or
four-up — band 2's four fields are a 2×2, because a 150px cell cannot hold
"Deterministic compiler (DIAMOND-46)". Shipping unused CSS in an email is bytes
for nothing.

### The colour law: "colour ENCODES, it never decorates"

Four hues, four jobs, no fifth:

| Hue | Its one job |
|---|---|
| **Lime `#C1FF72`** | the deterministic route and everything it produced |
| **Red `#FF5A47`** | the compile failed |
| **Amber `#E8C07D`** | the lead must act (the placeholder line, and nothing else) |
| **Yellow `#FFDD2F`** | the house — attribution only, never a data value |

Enforced by tests: a shipped order contains **no red anywhere**; a failed order
contains **no lime anywhere**; a clean build contains **no amber anywhere**; and
every line containing yellow is a gradient stop, the footer rule, or the house
attribution — never a value.

### Going a step past the reference, in three places

1. **The glow is the verdict, not decoration.** The reference glows each band a
   different hue — pretty, but at that point the hue is decorating. Here the glow
   is one hue, because the order has one outcome, and its intensity **swells on
   the band that states that outcome**: 0.09 on THE ORDER, **0.17 on THE BUILD**,
   0.07 on YOUR FILES. A shipped order is lit lime the whole way down; a failed
   one is lit red the whole way down. **The email is tinted by its own verdict.**
2. **The masthead ramp is lime-led, inverting the reference.** The Mavlers brief
   ramps yellow→lime because Mavlers is the sender there. Here **Maveloper** is
   the sender and Mavlers is the attribution, so the ramp starts on the product's
   hue and resolves into the house's: **lime → yellow**. The footer rule runs the
   other way (yellow → product) so the email opens on Maveloper and closes on the
   handoff back to it. On a failed order the ramp is red→yellow and the footer
   rule is yellow→red: **the house hue is the only thing constant across both
   variants, because it is the only thing that does not depend on this order.**
3. **`qbGradText_` is spent once, on the Order ID.** Not a numeric figure, but the
   one string the lead will quote back, search their inbox for and paste into a
   message — so it is the email's anchor. 30px, monospace, one solid-colour
   `<span>` per character ramping lime→yellow. Asserted: exactly 11 gradient spans
   for an 11-character id, and not one anywhere else.

### One real bug I introduced and fixed

The first draft coloured the link rows with the section accent. That meant a
**failed** order rendered its Figma link in failure red — telling the lead a link
that works perfectly is broken. Colour that encodes cannot be spent on a thing it
does not describe. **Links now carry no hue at all**: white, underlined, and the
word "Open". The single exception is the filled Dropbox button, which *is* the
verdict — and only exists on an order that shipped, so a lime button is always a
true statement. Both are tested.

### Still a real email

Table-based, `role="presentation"`, every style inline with explicit hex, reset
attributes alongside styles, no flexbox, no grid, no CSS custom properties, no
external stylesheet, no `border-radius`, an MSO conditional block, sharp edges.
The `<style>` block is progressive enhancement only — strip it and the email is
still correct at 600px.

**Light and dark:** the design is dark by intent, so a light client shows the dark
card as designed. A dark client that would re-colour our text is caught twice —
`prefers-color-scheme` **and** `[data-ogsc]`, because Outlook.com's dark mode
honours neither the former nor `@media`.

**When the webfont does not load:** it never loads. **No webfont is fetched at
all** — no `<link>`, no `@import` (asserted). Poppins is named first because Apple
Mail and iOS honour locally-installed fonts; `'Segoe UI'` is the deliberate second
because it is on every Windows machine and is what Gmail-on-Windows actually
draws. The weights, sizes and letter-spacing are chosen to hold up in Segoe UI and
Arial, which is the common case, not the fallback case.

## 7. ENCODED SIZE vs GMAIL'S CLIP THRESHOLD

Ported as `encodedSizeReport()`, and it runs on **every build**, not just in a
test — it costs a `Buffer.byteLength` and it is the difference between shipping a
clipped email and knowing about it. `diagnostics.encodedSize` carries the verdict.

Two encodings, because two are in play: quoted-printable at **+6%** (what the
reference checks, and what most ESPs emit) and base64 at **+33%** (what this
repo's own `buildRfc822` actually emits). **The verdict is taken against the worse
of the two.**

| | raw (html+text) | quoted-printable | base64 | worst case | vs 102,400 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Compiler | 22,979 | 24,209 | 30,452 | **30,452** | **NOT clipped — 70.3% headroom** |
| Failure | 19,859 | 20,902 | 26,292 | **26,292** | **NOT clipped — 74.3% headroom** |

The old `.eml` was 53,923 bytes largely because the preview PNG was base64'd into
it. That part is gone.
